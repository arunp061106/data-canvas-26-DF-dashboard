Tiruppur is known for its colours, but the catastrophe is underground.

Tiruppur powers approximately 90% of India's cotton knitwear exports, earning billions in foreign exchange. But decades of intensive dyeing, bleaching, and chemical processing have burdened the fragile Noyyal River basin. Untreated and partially treated industrial effluents carrying high concentrations of common salt (sodium chloride) and sodium sulphate historically drained into the Noyyal riverbed and the Orathupalayam reservoir, infiltrating shallow aquifers.

Today, the borewells and open dug wells that local families, farmers, and peri-urban communities rely upon are falling deeper into crystalline rock and turning brackish. This dashboard synthesizes verified groundwater monitoring records from the Central Ground Water Board (CGWB), high-frequency automated GPRS telemetry from India-WRIS, and gridded meteorological series from NASA POWER.

Hydrogeological Context

District Area: ~5,186 km² (Western Tamil Nadu).
Major Basin: Cauvery Basin • Noyyal & Amaravathi sub-basins.
Aquifer System: Charnockites, Gneisses, and Weathered Hard Rock crystalline aquifers.
Measurement Standard: Water table is strictly measured as Depth to Water Level in meters below ground level (m bgl). Higher values indicate deeper, more depleted water tables.

Scientific Integrity Disclosure: Multi-Year Trend Boundary

Notice on Multi-Year Comparisons: Proving that TDS is rising over multi-year periods requires repeated testing of identical wells across matching seasons over successive years. While the 2024 national publication includes Pre and Post-Monsoon samples for Tiruppur, Tiruppur district monitoring stations were omitted in the published CGWB 2023 national quality report (only Vellitirupur in neighbouring Erode appeared). The 2024 chemical records are therefore presented as a verified seasonal snapshot documenting severe local salinity and post-monsoon ionic concentration, rather than an unverified multi-year trend.

Methodological Guardrail: Gridded Estimates & Climate Teleconnections

1. NASA POWER Data: Represents a single-coordinate gridded estimate (11.1085°N, 77.3411°E), not an official IMD district-wide rain gauge network total.
2. RONI / ENSO Context: Relative Oceanic Niño Index anomalies track equatorial Pacific sea surface temperatures. While El Niño conditions historically suppress peninsular monsoon precipitation, RONI is presented as macro-climatic context and is not cited as mechanical proof that El Niño was the sole cause of localized well depletion.

About This Investigation

“A Catastrophe behind Colours” is a scientific open-data investigation into groundwater depletion and industrial salinization in Tiruppur and the Noyyal basin. Supporting United Nations Sustainable Development Goal 6 (Clean Water and Sanitation) and Goal 3 (Good Health and Well-Being).
